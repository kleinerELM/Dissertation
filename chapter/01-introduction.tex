\section{Background and Motivation}

Portland cement is the most commonly used binder for the production of concrete.
After adding water to the cement, a reaction called hydration begins.
The cement reactivity is determined by its composition and particle size.
During hydration, the cement begins to dissolve and precipitate new crystals that form a new microstructure and ultimately causes hardening of the cement paste.
This microstructure determines the properties of the resulting concrete.
A good knowledge of the structural development and therefore, reliable measurement and evaluation methods are crucial to understand, model \cite{nguyen-tuan.2023} and influence the hydration of cements.

This thesis is devoted to improve the characterization the micro-structure of unhydrated and hydrated cement based binders utilizing a set of imaging techniques like scanning electron microscopy and computer tomography.
The goal is to better understand the influence of the composition on the structure of hydration products.
Therefore, existing analytical methods had to be improved and further developed to be reproducible and automatable.

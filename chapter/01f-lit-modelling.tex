\section{Modelling of cement hydration and its reliance on \acs*{SEM} images}

% aus dem ct/fib-sem-antrag
As the demands on building materials increase, so do the requirements for characterising and modelling the underlying material systems.
There are advanced approaches to model and simulate the strength, shape retention, and durability characteristics of concretes \cite{bishnoi.2009, kumar.2017, zhou.2019, ouzia.2019, sac-morane.2026}.
Due to the high complexity, multi-scale heterogeneity and temporal changes, this is often only possible in a highly simplified model and on a very limited scale.

The microstructure of concretes was simulated in a variety of models at different scales \cite{bishnoi.2009, kumar.2017, zhou.2019, ouzia.2019}.
The mesostructure (between atomistic and micro scale) of e.g. \ac{CSH} phases and the porosity was also modelled \cite{ioannidou.2016, yu.2016}.
Atomistic modelling is used to fundamentally simulate the structural composition \cite{kumar.2017,mishra.2017,kunhimohamed.2020,casar.2023}.
Finally, there are modern micromechanical models that attempt to close the transition from meso- to macro-modelling \cite{bernard.2003, hlobil.2016, gobel.2018}.

The hydration of cement over time can also be simulated using the sheet growth model in 2D \cite{etzold.2014} and 3D \cite{nguyen-tuan.2019,nguyen-tuan.2023} (see \zcref{fig:sheet_growth}).

There also exists 3D simulation models.
\textsc{HYMOSTRUC} \cite{vanbreugel.1995} is a model to simulate the hydration of cement in 2D and has been further developed over the last few decades into \textsc{HYMOSTRUC3D} \cite{ye.2007, liu.2014, gao.2014, qian.2017, gao.2019}.
It is a vector-based model using spherical cement particles, growing shells around them.
In contrast, \textsc{CEMHYD3D} \cite{bentz.2005} is a voxel-based model to simulate the hydration of cement in 3D allowing irregularly shaped particles.
These models significantly differ in the simulated pore structure, as \textsc{HYMOSTRUC} results in a larger pore connectivity and relative diffusivity than in \textsc{CEMHYD3D}, caused by the simulation method \cite{gao.2013}.
Another more modern model is \textsc{HydraFE} \cite{nothnagel.2007} which is based on the finite-element-method, using an adaptive instead of a fixed grid as in \textsc{CEMHYD3D}.
The main advantage of \textsc{CEMHYD3D} is that its code is open source \cite{bentz.2005,gao.2013} in contrast to \textsc{HYMOSTRUC} and \textsc{HydraFE}.
However, today the code is only publicly accessible through the software package \textsc{SIFEL} \cite{kruis.2001, smilauer.2009}.
3D-based simulations inherently have a better chance to closely represent reality, since 2D simulations are always missing a lot of details, which can cause distorted results (see \zcref{subsec:sectioning-effect}).
However, 2D simulations are much faster and allow the simulation of much larger areas, reflecting a more representative specimen area, which is very important due to the heterogeneity of cement (see \zcref{sec:2D-representativeness}).

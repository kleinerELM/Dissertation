\section[Interfacial transition zone]{\Acl*{ITZ}}

Cement-based mortars and concretes are mixtures of aggregates and a cement matrix.
Around the interface between aggregates, walls, or reinforcement and the binder, a thin shell of a less dense matrix forms, known as the \ac{ITZ}.
It originates from the \textit{wall effect}, which leads to a depletion of larger cement particles in this area, resulting in a high-porosity zone \cite{gao.2014, torsaeter.2015}.
This higher porosity influences the mechanical properties \cite{zhu.2000,xiao.2013} and causes increased water permeability \cite{ye.2007}, making it the `weak link' in cementitious composites \cite{zhu.2000}.
Consequently, this zone is prone to microcracking \cite{ye.2007}.

The \ac{ITZ} is typically characterised by analysing the porosity around aggregates using \ac{SEM}-\ac{BSE} imaging \cite{gao.2014} or \ac{mCT} datasets \cite{lavrov.2017}, by measuring mechanical properties such as hardness and \textsc{Young}'s modulus via \ac{NI} \cite{zhu.2000,xiao.2013}, and through structural simulation \cite{ye.2007}.
However, the width of the \ac{ITZ} varies significantly in the literature as the selection in \zcref{tab:ITZ-width} shows.

This table shows that the \ac{ITZ} width typically ranges from \qtyrange{15}{60}{\micro\meter}.
It seems to be influenced by the interface material and the method used to measure it.
Approaches based on porosity or mechanical properties are the most common.
Using the density of remaining unhydrated cement particles, as proposed in \cite{torsaeter.2015}, appears to overestimate the \ac{ITZ} width and is impractical for real mortars or concretes.
A consistent observation in the literature comparing recycled and natural aggregates is that recycled aggregates result in an \ac{ITZ} that is \qtyrange{10}{20}{\micro\meter} wider.
This is probably one reason for the inferior mechanical properties of mortars and concretes made from recycled aggregates.
Furthermore, combined \ac{EDX} analysis revealed a \ce{Ca}-rich layer directly at the interface of the aggregates \cite{li.2012, tian.2018}.
This suggests the crystallisation of \ac{CH} in this region, a finding supported by \textit{HYMOSTRUC3D} simulations \cite{gao.2014}.

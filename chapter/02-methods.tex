\section{The preparation process for SEM imaging}

To obtain high-quality images using a \ac{SEM}, a lot of preparation methods are possible depending on the specimen type.
A selection of them is listet here:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Scatter samples}. This means sprinkling powdery material over an adhesive carbon pad, followed by the removal of loose material by using compressed air. This method is fairly dangerous for the \ac{SEM} if not done properly.
	\item \textbf{Fractured surface}. Crack the specimen, lean it with compressed air and observe the freshly prepared surface.
	\item \textbf{Cross section (large scale)}. Most mineral specimens have to be embedded in epoxy resin and are then mechanically polished.
	\item \textbf{\Ac{FIB} cross section}. Cutting and polishing a small surface (up to 40 $\times$ \SI{10}{\micro\metre}) using \ac{FIB}
	\item \textbf{Cryo preparation}. ...
\end{itemize}

The standard, polished cross section is often used to obtain \ac{BSE} images.
However, to do a quantitave image analysis, very smooth surfaces are required.

\subsection{mechanical polishing}

Those surfaces can be achieved by mechanically polishing epoxy embedded specimens.
The epoxy resin need to be of a very low viscosity to deeply penetrate the specimen and fill the majority of pores.
In this work, a combination of the resin R1370 and the hardener R1376, by Agar Scientific was used.

To embed the specimen, it has first to be dried at \SIrange{40}{60}{\celsius}, depending on the sensitivity of the specimen, in an oven for \SI{12}{\hour}.
The sample, which has been brought to the correct size, is now poured over with epoxy resin in a mold.
This is followed by degassing in two \SI{30}{\min} cycles.

After hardening for \SI{24}{\hour} in an oven at \SI{40}{\celsius}, the specimen can be polished.
This is done in multiple steps using diamond-oil-slurries.
The particle sizes of the diamond pastes will be sequentially reduced (15, 9, 3, 1 down to \SI{0.25}{\micro\metre}).
While this polishing method is good enough for many applications, high-resolution imaging or \ac{EBSD} require even better surface qualities.

\subsection{Argon Broad Ion Beam Polishing (moving specimen)}

Surface polishing using argon ions can be applied to improve mechanically polished specimens or to remove thin coatings.
To polish a large area, the specimen is semi-erratically moved under a grazing incidence angle of three ion beams.

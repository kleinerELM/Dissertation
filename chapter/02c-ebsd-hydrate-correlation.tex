\section{Correlation of hydrate rim measurements with \acs*{EBSD} orientations}
\label{sec:ebsd-hydrate}

To relate the \ac{HLT} measurements as described in \zcref{subsec:algo_hlm} to the crystallographic orientation of the same clinker volume, the \ac{BSE} images can be combined with \ac{EBSD} data acquired on the identical surface of a polished hydrated \ac{C3S} sample.

To measure the crystal orientation, the carbon-coated specimen was mounted on a pre-tilted specimen holder (\qty{45}{\degree}) and the surface was then tilted to a \qty{70}{\degree} angle to the electron beam (see \zcref{fig:ebsd_principle_a}).
The specimen was then scanned at \qty{15}{\kilo\volt}, \qty{1.6}{\nano\ampere} at a step size of \qty{2}{\micro\meter\per\px} to acquire the \textsc{Kikuchi} patterns of \ac{C3S} and \ac{CH}.

The \ac{EBSD} experiment produces a 2D dataset of crystal orientation estimates, phase indices, and pattern quality indicators \cite{kleiner.2026a}. 
The raw data were preprocessed in \textit{AZtecCrystal 3.2} \cite{azteccrystal.2010} by \textsc{Oxford Instruments} to clean up pseudosymmetric solutions.
Since the crystallinity of \ac{C2S} is not good enough to obtain usable \textsc{Kikuchi} patterns, this work focussed on hydrated \ac{C3S} specimens \cite{rossler.2022}.
To identify and fit the patterns of \ac{C3S}, the crystal structure published by \textcite{delatorre.2002} (\acs{ICSD} 94742, \acs{COD} \href{https://qiserver.ugr.es/cod/1540705.html}{1540705}) was used (see \zcref{fig:c3s_unit_cell}).
Finally, the measured \ac{EBSD} data were exported as a channel text file (.ctf) \cite{kleiner.2026a}, which was further processed using \textit{Python}.

The \ac{BSE} image was manually aligned to the \ac{EBSD} map (using the band-contrast image) in \textit{QGIS 3.42} \cite{QGIS_software} by identifying common points \cite{kleiner.2026a}.
The \textit{QGIS}-project was then used to projectively transform the high-resolution \ac{BSE} image to the low-resolution \ac{EBSD} dataset in \textit{Python}.

%After alignment, the physical step sizes of the \ac{EBSD} grid and the transformed \ac{BSE} image were harmonised by an integer upscale of the segmentation used in the rim algorithm and a scalar alignment factor between low-resolution and high-resolution \ac{BSE} exports, so that one \ac{EBSD} column and row index corresponds to a consistent footprint on the section.

After the data alignment, real-world data exhibit that not all pixels assigned to \ac{C3S} also have a corresponding usable pixel with good \ac{EBSD} data.
Furthermore, combining the clinker mask from the segmented \ac{BSE} image often results in a \qty{1}{\px} shift and, therefore, unassigned areas.
Since this issue would significantly reduce the measurement density, the available \ac{EBSD} data of a particle were expanded until all pixels assigned to a \ac{C3S} particle had a corresponding crystal orientation (see \zcref{fig:clinker-ebsd-fill}).
For every \ac{CSH} rim measurement, the clinker-hydrate boundary point extracted from the \ac{BSE} segmentation was projected onto the nearest \ac{EBSD} grid node.
The \textsc{Euler} angles at that node represent the orientation attributed to the measurement.
Where several angular steps around one particle map to the same grid node, multiple records can share identical indices. %; this is expected when the \ac{EBSD} step width is large compared with the arc length sampled along the rim.

As schematically illustrated in \zcref{fig:ebsd-hydrate-correlation}, in the polar representation of the rim algorithm (\zcref{fig:hlt-method}), each valid direction is described by an azimuth $\theta_\mathrm{idx}$ in the plane of the sample surface.
A unit vector $\vec{n}_\mathrm{sample}$ was constructed in the sample reference frame with components
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:nsample-plane}
	\vec{n}_\mathrm{sample} = \bigl(\cos\theta,\; \sin\theta,\; 0\bigr)^\mathrm{T},
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ follows from the discrete index and an optional sign convention for the image $y$-axis.

The crystal orientation at the associated grid point yields $G(\varphi_1,\,\Phi,\,\varphi_2)$ in the \textsc{Bunge} convention ($G = R_{z}(\varphi_2)\,R_{x}(\Phi)\,R_{z}(\varphi_1)$).
For the monoclinic unit cell of \ce{C3S}, the sample-space direction $\vec{n}_\mathrm{sample}$ must first be expressed in the crystal's fractional lattice basis, since the crystallographic axes are not orthogonal.
This is accomplished with the inverse unit-cell transformation matrix $M^{-1}$ (\zcref{eq:monoclinic-Minv}), which maps a Cartesian direction into monoclinic fractional coordinates.
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:monoclinic-Minv}
    M^{-1} =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1/a & 0 & -\frac{\cos\beta}{a\sin\beta} \\
        0   & 1/b & 0 \\
        0   & 0 & \frac{1}{c\sin\beta}
    \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
\begin{conditions}
	a, b, c, \beta	&	cell parameters of the crystal \\
\end{conditions}

The corresponding rim direction in the crystal reference frame is then
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:h-crystal}
    \vec{h} = \frac{G\,M^{-1}\,\vec{n}_\mathrm{sample}}{\lVert G\,\bigl(M^{-1}\,\vec{n}_\mathrm{sample}\bigr)\rVert},
\end{equation}
with components $(h_x,\,h_y,\,h_z)^\mathrm{T}$.
Because $\vec{h}$ is normalised, all measurements lie on the unit sphere in crystal coordinates, which allows a later visualisation.

For the monoclinic clinker phase investigated here, the angle between the measured rim normal direction and the $\langle001\rangle$ family was summarised by
\begin{equation}
	\label{eq:angle-001}
	\alpha_{001} = \arccos\bigl(\lvert h_z\rvert\bigr),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the acute angle between $\vec{h}$ and the crystal $c$-axis, using the absolute value of $h_z$ so that opposite poles of the same axis are treated equivalently in the statistical plots.
The rim length from the hydrate analysis was retained alongside $\alpha_{001}$ and $(h_x,\,h_y,\,h_z)$ for joint distributions, spatial maps on $(i_x,\,i_y)$, and spherical heat maps of measurement density on the orientation sphere.

The phase map delivered with the \ac{EBSD} data does not always label every pixel that appears as clinker in the \ac{BSE} segmentation. 
This can happen when pattern indexing fails at the particle boundary or when there is a slight misalignment between the datasets.
To reduce missing orientations at valid rim locations, a correction was applied on the \ac{EBSD} grid.
Pixels that fell inside a clinker mask derived from the upscaled \ac{BSE} segmentation but were not indexed as clinker in the raw \ac{EBSD} data were assigned the \textsc{Euler} angles of the nearest indexed clinker pixel within the same connected clinker region.
Pixels outside a defined radius were left unchanged to avoid interpolation across unrelated particles.

Measurements from several specimen positions were concatenated into a single table and analysed together to improve the data density.

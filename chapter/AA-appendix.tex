\section{Freezing point depression due to \ce{CaOH}}
\label{sec:freezing-point-depression}

%\reaction[CaOH_dissolution]{Ca(OH2)<=>Ca2++2OH-}



The cryoscopic constant $K_{f}$ of water is \qty{1.86}{\celsius\kilo\gram\per\mole} and the solubility product $K_{sp}$ of \ce{Ca(OH)2} is \num{5.02e-6} \cite{haynes.2017}.
\begin{equation}
    K_{sp} = [\text{Ca}^{2+}][\text{OH}^{-}]^2 = (S)(2S)^2 = 4S^3
\end{equation}

The molar solubility $S$ can now be calculated as:
\begin{equation}
	S = \sqrt[3]{ \frac{K_{sp}}{4}} = \sqrt[3]{ \frac{\num{5.02e-6}}{4}} = 0.01079 \frac{\text{mol}}{\text{L}}
\end{equation}

The mass of \qty{1}{\litre} of the saturated \ce{Ca(OH)2}-solution is \qty{0.999}{\kilo\gram\per\liter}.
The molality $m$ of \ce{Ca(OH)2} at its saturation point ($n=S$) at room temperature is therefore:
\begin{equation}
	m = \frac{n}{M}  = \frac{0.01079 \frac{\text{mol}}{\text{L}}}{0.999\frac{\text{kg}}{\text{L}}} = 0.01080 \frac{\text{mol}}{\text{kg}}
\end{equation}

The temperature depression can now be calculated as 
\begin{equation}
	\Delta T_f = i_\text{vH} \cdot K_f \cdot m = 3 \cdot 1.86 \frac{^\circ\text{C} \cdot \text{kg}}{\text{mol}} \cdot 0.01080 \frac{\text{mol}}{\text{kg}}
\end{equation}
\begin{conditions}
	i_\text{vH}	&	\textsc{van 't-Hoff} factor, here 3 (number of ions in \ce{Ca(OH)2})
\end{conditions}
\begin{equation}
	\Delta T_f = \qty{0.0602}{\celsius}
\end{equation}

The freezing point of a saturated \ce{Ca(OH)2}-solution is therefore to be expected at \qty{-0.0602}{\celsius}.
